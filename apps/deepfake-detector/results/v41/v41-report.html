1. Executive Summary

Key finding: V4.1 did not improve generalization to unseen XTTS voices. On the key unseen-synthetic test set, V4.1 and V4-A both achieved 4/7 (57.1%). This experiment therefore did not demonstrate that the added XTTS voice diversity was sufficient to learn a generalizable XTTS-specific signal.

V4.1 tested one specific expansion: adding 10 XTTS clone_speaker examples from 6 new speakers to the training set (alongside 18 new natural voice recordings). The hypothesis was that exposing the model to XTTS clones from diverse source voices would teach it to recognize XTTS-the-engine rather than specific XTTS voice timbres.

The experiment used a voice-disjoint split: no speaker appeared in both training and test sets. Four test speakers (foxtrot, golf, juliet, mike) were held out entirely, with their XTTS clones forming the key unseen-synthetic test set.

Production decision: V4-A remains the selected booth model. Its combination of zero false-positive rate on humans and acceptable in-distribution performance makes it the safest choice for a conference demo where falsely accusing a human is worse than missing a synthetic clip.

9. The Deployed Model Trade-off

The currently deployed model (wav2vec2-heavy-augmented) scores the highest on unseen-synthetic detection (71.4%, 5/7). However, this comes at a severe cost in false-positive behavior.

Deployed Model Strengths

  • Unseen synthetic: 71.4% (5/7) — best of all three
  • In-distribution val: 89.6% balanced accuracy
  • Detects foxtrot-cs (p=0.505) that V4-A and V4.1 miss

Deployed Model Failures

  • Same-speaker holdout: 50.0% balanced accuracy
  • Calls jakub's real voice fake (p=0.757)
  • Calls jveverka real voice fake (p=0.969)
  • Unseen natural: 83.3% (2 false positives)
  • Val FPR: 12.5% vs. V4-A's 0%
The 50% same-speaker result exposes the deployed model's strategy: it is trigger-happy, not perceptive. It detects more XTTS clones only because it calls more things fake in general. At a conference booth, this means falsely accusing real human speakers of being AI — a much worse user experience than missing a synthetic clip.

12. Limitations and Open Questions

What this experiment did NOT establish

This experiment tested one particular expansion — 10 additional XTTS clone examples from 6 speakers — and that was not sufficient. It does not establish that the entire fine-tuning approach cannot work with more data, different augmentation, or different layer-freezing strategies.
  • Small test set: 7 unseen-synthetic clips from 4 speakers is not statistically robust. A single speaker (juliet) accounts for both misses. One more detected clip would change the headline from 57% to 71%.
  • XTTS-only: The experiment tests only XTTS clone_speaker. Fish Audio S2 Pro clones were not tested in the unseen-voice regime. Fish detection may also have unseen-speaker weaknesses.
  • No Slovak TTS: XTTS does not support Slovak, creating a language gap. Slovak speakers received Czech-language clones, which may confound language and generator signals.
  • Single sentence: All XTTS clones in the test set use the same text. Text-specific artifacts could inflate or deflate detection rates.
  • Juliet anomaly: The complete failure on juliet across all models warrants investigation. Is this a speaker whose voice characteristics happen to mask XTTS artifacts, or is there a systematic property (e.g., recording quality, pitch range) that correlates with evasion?

Observations Worth Noting

  • V4.1 achieved 100% accuracy on unseen natural speakers (vs. V4-A's 91.7%), suggesting the additional natural voice diversity genuinely improved real-voice generalization, even though it did not improve synthetic detection.
  • The jakub holdout results are identical between V4-A and V4.1 (80.8%), indicating the expanded dataset neither helped nor hurt same-speaker detection.
  • V4.1 moved juliet-cs p_fake from 0.291 to 0.427 — closer to the threshold but not enough. With a lower threshold (e.g., 0.4), V4.1 would detect 5/7 unseen clones. Threshold tuning was not explored.

13. Next Research Direction

The V4.1 result raises a question about whether Wav2Vec2 fine-tuning can capture the XTTS clone_speaker artifact signal at all, or whether a different representation or detection approach is needed.

Possible directions (not prioritized for the current demo)

DirectionRationaleEffort
More XTTS clone training data 10 examples may be insufficient. 50+ clones from 20+ speakers could surface the engine signature that 10 could not. Low — requires speaker collection and XTTS generation only
Different detection representation Wav2Vec2 may not represent the artifacts that distinguish XTTS clones. Spectrogram-based models, MFCC features, or purpose-built deepfake detectors may capture different signal. Medium — architecture exploration
Threshold tuning V4.1 nearly detects juliet-cs at threshold 0.4. A calibrated threshold per-generator could improve recall without sacrificing FPR on real clips. Low — but risks false positives
Multi-model ensemble Combine Wav2Vec2 (good at Fish) with a spectrogram model (potentially better at XTTS clone artifacts) High — inference latency doubles
Adversarial data augmentation Train with augmented XTTS samples (pitch shift, speed change) to force the model to learn structural rather than surface artifacts Medium
For the current booth demo: V4-A is sufficient. The XTTS clone blind spot is a known limitation that does not affect the game (clips are pre-loaded from known speakers). Research into alternative detection approaches should wait until after the conference demo is stable in production.
